Welcome to my website! I am a current PhD student in the Department of Agricultural and Resource Economics at the [University of Connecticut](https://are.uconn.edu). 

I am an applied microeconomist studying consumer behavior, market responses, and welfare, with applications to food, agricultural, and health markets. My research interests lie at the intersection of agricultural economics and empirical industrial organization.

My current research examines how consumers respond to retail beef price increases and the heterogeneous substitution patterns and welfare effects across income groups. I also study how agricultural producers respond to ecological and local technology shocks, including how cocoa diseases affect productivity and input use among farmers in Ghana and how wind farms influence agricultural productivity and land-use decisions in the United States. In related industrial organization research, I study markups in retail markets and market power in the US meatpacking industry.

Across these projects, I combine structural and reduced-form approaches with tools from causal inference, spatial econometrics, and machine learning, drawing on administrative, survey, and high-resolution geospatial data.

Outside of research, I enjoy running, watching documentaries, and reading crime fiction.
